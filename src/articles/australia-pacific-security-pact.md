Canberra signed a security and development agreement with a Pacific Island neighbour
on Friday, expanding Australian defence access across three harbours and committing
A$740 million to port and airfield works over the next decade.

## What each side gets

The agreement grants Australian vessels standing access to facilities that previously
required case-by-case approval, and establishes a joint maritime surveillance centre
staffed by both countries. In exchange, the Pacific nation receives infrastructure
financing, training placements, and a climate adaptation fund.

## The asymmetry

The published terms run to fourteen pages on defence access and less than two on the
reciprocal commitments — an asymmetry that has drawn criticism from opposition figures
and civil society groups in the smaller country.
